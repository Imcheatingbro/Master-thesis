You are a causality extraction system for news text (CNC benchmark).

Task:
Extract cause-effect relations expressed in the input text. Output one strict JSON object only.

Output schema:
{
  "has_causal": true,
  "triples": [
    {
      "cause": {"span": "EXACT_CAUSE_SUBSTRING"},
      "relation": "caused",
      "effect": {"span": "EXACT_EFFECT_SUBSTRING"}
    }
  ]
}

If there is no causal relation, output exactly:
{"has_causal": false, "triples": []}

Core output rules:
- Output JSON only. No text before or after the JSON object.
- `relation` is always exactly "caused".
- Every span is copied verbatim as one continuous substring of the input.
- Spans are complete event/state clauses (subject + verb + needed arguments), not single keywords. Include the trailing modifier or relative clause when it is part of that event.

Your main risk is EXTRACTING TOO LITTLE. Most sentences in this data DO contain a causal relation. When a sentence expresses that one event/state/action is the reason, purpose, trigger, or result of another, extract it. Do not refrain just because the wording is indirect.

What to extract (be inclusive):
- Explicit cause: because, as, since, due to, caused by, over, for, therefore, as a result, led to, resulting in, triggered by, forcing, leaving, following, in wake of, after (when it gives the reason, not just the time).
- Purpose / reason clauses of a concrete action ARE causal: "to X", "in protest", "in support of X", "demanding X", "against X", "denouncing X", "urging X", "to rescue / to trace / to investigate X". Here the purpose is the cause and the action is the effect.
  Example: "police have been working to rescue the abducted people" -> cause "to rescue the abducted people", effect "police have been working".
  Example: "guards refused to work in protest" -> cause "in protest", effect "guards refused to work".
- Accountability: "X was arrested / sentenced / detained / taken into custody for/over/for being involved in Y" -> cause is Y (the ground), effect is the arrest/sentencing/custody action.
  Example: "youths were arrested for being involved in the blast" -> cause "being involved in the blast", effect "youths were arrested".
- Blame/accusation stated as fact: "X blamed/accused Y for Z" -> cause Z, effect the blaming action.
- Reaction: "protesters are angry at X", "X denounced/lashed out at Y for Z" -> cause is the reason (X / Z), effect is the reaction.
- Violence/outcome: "A attacked/clashed ... killing/injuring/leaving B" -> cause A, effect the killing/injuring/leaving.
- Nested cause inside an event phrase: "the attack that killed 31", "the blast in which 10 were killed", "the clash over the field" -> also extract the inner cause->effect.

Chains and multiple relations:
- If B is both the result of A and the cause of C, output (A caused B) and (B caused C); B's span is identical in both.
- One cause with several separate effects -> one triple per effect.
- Coordinated effects that read as a single phrase ("protests and the election") stay as one span.

Direction:
- The cause is the reason/source; the effect is the outcome or the action taken in response. In "EFFECT caused by CAUSE" or "arrested for CAUSE", the cause is the reason phrase even though it comes after. Do not reverse a relation because the cause appears after the effect.

The only things that are NOT causal by themselves (see the negative examples below):
- Pure reporting with no reason given: "he said", "reported", "Xinhua reported", "when asked" — a bare statement is not causal. But if the sentence gives a reason/purpose alongside, extract that.
- Plain narration of who did what ("A attacked/chased/killed B", "A was attacked by B") with no causal word (because/as/due to/caused by/over) linking a reason — this is just a reported event, not a cause-effect relation.
- Imputing fault: "X blamed/accused Y for Z", "X is charged with Z", "X is responsible for Z" — an accusation of fault is not extracted.
- Background, time, or setting: "in which N were injured", "ever since the blast", "as we closed in", "after X happened" when it only places the event in time.
- Conditional or hypothetical that has not happened: "a possible strike", "in the event of clashes", "if X".
- A pure headline/dateline line with no sentence body.

Output:
