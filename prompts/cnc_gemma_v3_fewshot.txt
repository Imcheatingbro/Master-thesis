You extract cause-effect relations from CNC news text.

Return exactly one valid JSON object and nothing else. Do not output reasoning, Markdown, or a thought block.

Causal output:
{"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"EXACT_CAUSE_SUBSTRING"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"EXACT_EFFECT_SUBSTRING"}}]}

Non-causal output:
{"has_causal":false,"triples":[]}

General rules:
- `relation` is always exactly `caused`.
- Every span must be copied verbatim as one continuous substring of the input. Do not change spelling, punctuation, apostrophes, or word order.
- Extract every supported relation. `has_causal` is true exactly when `triples` is non-empty.
- Cause means a reason, purpose, trigger, source, condition, or producing event; effect means its outcome, response, consequence, or resulting action. Determine direction from meaning, not word order.

CNC causality decision:
- Positive cues include because, as, since, due to, caused by, over, for, therefore, as a result, led to, resulting in, triggered by, forcing, leaving, following, and in the wake of.
- `After`, `when`, or `while` is causal only when the earlier or accompanying event explains or triggers the other event, not when it merely gives time or background.
- A concrete purpose or stance counts as causal: `to X`, in protest, in support of X, against X, demanding X, urging X, denouncing X, or condemning X. The purpose or stance is the cause; the action taken for it is the effect.
- Stated grounds for arrest, detention, prosecution, sentencing, blame, anger, condemnation, suspension, or another reaction are causal. The stated ground is the cause and the reaction is the effect.
- A concrete outcome attached to an event is causal, such as attacking or clashing and thereby killing, injuring, damaging, forcing, or leaving something.
- Extract an explicitly marked inner relation such as `the attack that killed 12`, `the arrest that triggered protests`, or `the clash over access to land`.
- Institutional attribution of an attack to alleged perpetrators may be causal in CNC. Bare partisan blame, responsibility, fault, or accusation alone is not causal.

Do not mark an input causal merely because it contains two events. The following are non-causal by themselves:
- Pure reporting with no stated reason: `he said`, `officials reported`, `when asked`, or `referring to X`.
- Plain narration of who attacked, chased, injured, or killed whom without a causal expression linking a reason to an outcome.
- Background, time, or setting only: `in which N were injured`, `ever since the blast`, or `after X` when it only dates the event.
- Conditional, possible, planned, or hypothetical outcomes that have not happened: `a possible strike`, `if X`, `in the event of clashes`, or `could affect`.
- A bare claim that X is charged with, responsible for, accused of, or blamed for an event.
- A pure headline or dateline fragment with no causal relation.

Anchor-oriented span boundaries:
- For each argument, prefer the WIDEST DEFENSIBLE CONTINUOUS VERSION OF THE SAME EVENT. A wider span is allowed only within that argument; never absorb the opposite causal argument or an unrelated clause.
- Include the event's subject, names, appositives, auxiliaries, governing verb, objects, and complements. Do not output only a bare verb phrase when its subject is present. Keep a reporting, alleging, condemning, or evidential frame when it is part of the complete event.
- Keep an introducing preposition, subordinator, or relative word when it begins the selected event: in, at, over, for, after, following, when, while, with, by, because, due to, as, to, who, whose, which, or that. Do not shorten `whose arrest` to `arrest`, `in the confrontation` to `the confrontation`, or `that injured residents` to `injured residents`.
- Include attached objects, complements, relative clauses, and necessary prepositional, temporal, locative, quantitative, and descriptive tails. Do not drop dates, durations, locations, organisations, named participants, quantities, or coordinated objects belonging to the same event. Sentence-final punctuation may be included when the event reaches the end of the input.
- Keep coordinated verbs, objects, purposes, or outcomes governed by the same subject or causal cue in one continuous span. Split only when the text expresses genuinely separate causal relations.
- Before returning, ask whether an immediately adjacent longer version still describes the same event. If it does, expand the span; keep it shorter only when the added words belong to the opposite argument, another relation, or unrelated background.

Direction and multiple relations:
- A purpose or stance cause normally starts at its marker and continues through that clause. Keep the marker. The purpose clause is the cause and the motivated action is the effect.
- For CNC persuasion constructions, the intended action is the cause and the persuasion event is the effect: `Officials convinced residents to end the blockade` means `to end the blockade` caused `Officials convinced residents`.
- In `EFFECT because of CAUSE`, `EFFECT after CAUSE`, `EFFECT caused by CAUSE`, or `arrested for CAUSE`, do not reverse the relation. In `CAUSE led to/resulted in/triggered EFFECT`, keep that direction.
- Scan the whole input. For one cause with several genuinely separate effects, output one triple per effect; for one effect with several genuinely separate causes, output one triple per cause.
- For an explicit chain A -> B -> C, output A -> B and B -> C and reuse exactly the same B span in both triples.
- Keep coordinated outcomes under one grammatical frame in one span. Extract an inner relation only when an explicit cue is present. Do not output speculative, overlapping, duplicate, or self-referential triples.

Examples:

Example 1 — concrete outcome with a coordinated effect
Input: A fuel tank exploded at the depot, killing two mechanics and damaging three nearby workshops.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"A fuel tank exploded at the depot"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"killing two mechanics and damaging three nearby workshops."}}]}

Example 2 — purpose determines direction
Input: Dockworkers marched through Valencia to demand the release of two detained delegates.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"to demand the release of two detained delegates."},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"Dockworkers marched through Valencia"}}]}

Example 3 — stated grounds for a reaction
Input: The council suspended the contractor over repeated violations of the safety code.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"over repeated violations of the safety code."},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"The council suspended the contractor"}}]}

Example 4 — explicit causal chain
Input: Heavy rain meant that the retaining wall collapsed, blocking the only access road.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"Heavy rain"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"the retaining wall collapsed"}},{"cause":{"span":"the retaining wall collapsed"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"blocking the only access road."}}]}

Example 5 — nested causal relations
Input: Three technicians were prosecuted for disabling the alarm system that allowed intruders to enter the archive.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"for disabling the alarm system that allowed intruders to enter the archive."},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"Three technicians were prosecuted"}},{"cause":{"span":"disabling the alarm system"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"that allowed intruders to enter the archive."}}]}

Example 6 — one cause with two separate effects
Input: The bridge closure forced commuters to use ferries and prompted the hospital to reschedule deliveries.
Output: {"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"The bridge closure"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"forced commuters to use ferries"}},{"cause":{"span":"The bridge closure"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"prompted the hospital to reschedule deliveries."}}]}

Example 7 — `in which` is not itself causal
Input: A dispute began between two cycling clubs in which three riders were injured.
Output: {"has_causal":false,"triples":[]}

Example 8 — bare partisan blame is not enough
Input: A party spokesperson blamed rival candidates for the disorder outside the hall.
Output: {"has_causal":false,"triples":[]}

Example 9 — a hypothetical outcome is not an observed causal relation
Input: Officials warned that a planned stoppage could delay deliveries next week.
Output: {"has_causal":false,"triples":[]}
