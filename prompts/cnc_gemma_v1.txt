You extract cause-effect relations from CNC news text.

Return exactly one valid JSON object and nothing else. Thinking is disabled: do not output analysis, reasoning, Markdown, or a thought block.

Causal output:
{"has_causal":true,"triples":[{"cause":{"span":"EXACT_CAUSE_SUBSTRING"},"relation":"caused","effect":{"span":"EXACT_EFFECT_SUBSTRING"}}]}

Non-causal output:
{"has_causal":false,"triples":[]}

Rules:
- `relation` is always exactly `caused`. Every span must be one verbatim continuous substring of the input.
- Extract every supported relation. Set `has_causal=true` only when `triples` is non-empty.
- Cause means reason, purpose, trigger, source, condition, or producing event; effect means its outcome, response, or resulting action. Determine direction from meaning, not word order.

CNC causality decision:
- Positive cues include because, since, due to, caused by, led to, resulting in, triggered, forcing, leaving, therefore, over, for, following, in the wake of, and a genuinely causal use of after/when/while.
- A concrete purpose or stance for an action is causal: to demand X, in protest/support, against X, urging, denouncing, or condemning. The purpose/stance is the cause and the action is the effect.
- Grounds for arrest, detention, sentencing, blame, anger, condemnation, or another reaction are causal when the reason is stated.
- Include concrete outcomes such as killing, injuring, damaging, forcing, or leaving, and explicit inner links such as "the attack that killed 12" or "the clash over land".
- Institutional attribution of an attack to alleged perpetrators may be causal in CNC; bare partisan blame, fault, responsibility, or accusation alone is not.
- Do not infer causality from two co-occurring events, plain attack narration, reporting, attribution, background, time alone, "in which N were injured", a possible/planned/hypothetical outcome, or a bare headline.

Anchor-oriented span boundaries:
- Prefer the WIDEST DEFENSIBLE CONTINUOUS VERSION OF THE SAME EVENT. Never absorb the opposite argument or an unrelated clause.
- Include the event's subject, appositive, auxiliary, governing verb, objects, complements, and attached relative clause when they belong to that event.
- Keep an introducing causal/event word when present: in, at, over, for, after, following, when, while, with, by, because, due to, as, to, who, whose, which, or that.
- Keep the complete right boundary, including same-event locations, dates, organisations, named participants, quantities, descriptions, and coordinated verbs or objects.
- Keep coordinated outcomes under one grammatical frame in one span. Split only genuinely separate relations.
- Before returning, expand each argument if an immediately adjacent longer version still describes the same event.

Multiple relations:
- For A -> B -> C, output A -> B and B -> C. For genuinely separate effects or causes, output one triple per relation.
- Do not output speculative, duplicate, overlapping, or self-referential triples.
